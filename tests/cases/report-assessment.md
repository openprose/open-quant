# Distinguishing assessment outcomes

These are authored review cases for [the operating-report assessment program](../../examples/assess-report.md). They are not model-produced judgments or a reliability benchmark. Read the selected subject agreement; the table does not add requirements to an unrelated invocation.

| Supplied situation | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| A monitoring report correctly records the breach and unsupported closure, with complete content and result identities, but no execution trace is supplied. | Content can be met while execution effects and overall subject fulfillment remain unresolved. The assessment can be fulfilled if it completed all its own required checks and reported the limits. |
| The report falsely claims I1 is closed because an action-complete date exists; effects evidence is also unavailable. | The known content violation makes overall subject fulfillment not met. Unresolved effects do not erase the violation. |
| A complete report says the underlying control breached its limit. | The adverse control finding is not itself a report violation. The reporting agreement asks for an accurate review, not an all-clear result. |
| The caller selected `shape-and-locality`, but the subject records `shape` and claims B meets all selected numerical requirements. | Expose the binding conflict and assess the caller-selected agreement; B's supplied locality value does not meet the additional requirement. Do not silently accept the subject's narrower mode. |
| The original policy hash differs from the current file, and the original bytes are unavailable. | Identify the unreconstructible agreement and affected checks as unfinished. Do not apply current policy retroactively or claim complete assessment coverage. |
| One decisive subject violation is found, but other required checks are not performed. | A negative subject conclusion may be supported while this complete-assessment program remains unfulfilled. Finding a defect is not completion of every assessment requirement. |
| The evaluator gives a correct negative finding but omits required subject hashes and requirement identities. | The conclusion does not excuse omitted assessment requirements. Its own fulfillment claim must reflect the missing work. |
| The evaluator recommends adding a citation for an observation absent from the evidence. | A locator alone cannot repair missing support. Identify the missing observation or other substantive evidence needed, without fabricating it or taking an unauthorized action. |

A source-preservation claim needs appropriate execution evidence. A hash in the subject's result is not proof of its actual runtime input, and a plausible report is not proof that every permission was respected. Conversely, unavailable effect evidence must not be mislabeled as an observed forbidden action.

## Full methodology-document assessment

The [full-document assessment entry](../../examples/sofr-documentation/assess.md) has a separate three-artifact subject: document.md, reviews.md and result.md. These additional cases are authored expectations, not evaluator observations.

- A document satisfies D1–D10 under the declared public scope, including disclosure of the three institutional gaps. Those gaps do not violate D9, which asks for disclosure. They also do not establish the missing facts or institutional readiness. Unobserved execution effects remain unresolved independently of content.
- The missing-locality document accurately states that the required measurements are unavailable. Its disclosure is correct, but D5 still is not met. A complete, accurate supporting audit may report that negative finding; its completeness does not make the document complete.
- The document has all required content, but its citation audit merely lists sources without checking applicability/support. Document content cannot excuse the separate report obligation. A correct high-level conclusion does not fill an unfinished audit.
- The document was changed after the recorded readiness review, and the assessed earlier revision cannot be reconstructed. A matching current source register does not bind that earlier judgment to the new artifact. Identify the stale identity and unfinished re-assessment rather than transferring acceptance.
- A choice register marks an unsupplied economic rationale as absent. That can meet this example's disclosure requirement; inventing a plausible reason cannot. A different agreement requiring a substantiated rationale would remain unmet until that evidence is supplied.
