# Review brief

Review six retrospective selectors over thirteen synthetic feature records and ten requested decisions. The question is whether each observed selection is supported by the supplied historical identity and timing evidence, and whether the prediction-error comparison preserves its population. This is not a production forecasting record or a proposed investment strategy.

A fixed toy prediction copies a uniquely selected dimensionless feature value. Corrected/latest values deliberately equal the later synthetic outcomes. Perfect error therefore cannot establish forecasting ability. Multiple decisions can concern the same entity and period; they are not independent trials.

Each packet retains recorded source IDs, selection states, predictions and errors. A recorded state of `selected` means one record was chosen, not that the choice satisfies the selected history policy. The receipt identifies the original development calculation; it does not provide a missing exact timestamp or replace the independent review requested here.

Account for every decision for each selector, with concise grouped findings where useful. Explain publication versus local availability, revision precedence, observation-date identity, exact cutoff boundaries and unresolved source conflicts. Preserve what is still known when a timestamp is unavailable. A source value may have been available through a different receipt even when the selected receipt's lineage is unsupported; do not equate those claims.
