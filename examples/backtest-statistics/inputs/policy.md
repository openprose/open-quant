# Selected statistical questions

The frequency null is independent Bernoulli trials with constant exception probability p0=1/100. For each aggregate K of N=250, review the one-sided exact excess-frequency probability P(X≥K). The supplied native calculation uses alternative `greater`; compare it with the finite binomial sum to absolute tolerance 1e-12. This question does not test whether forecasts are excessively conservative.

Separately review the two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson probability interval, to absolute endpoint tolerance 1e-10 against finite-sum inversion. Its lower endpoint solves Pp(X≥K)=0.025 and its upper solves Pp(X≤K)=0.025, with the usual zero/all-exception boundaries. This interval is not the inversion of the selected one-sided 5% test. An interval describes the procedure's repeated-sample coverage under its assumptions, not a posterior probability for the parameter. Zero exceptions do not establish zero exception probability.

For timing, R is the number of consecutive blocks of ones, including a block starting at position one. Conditional on N,K under the independent constant-probability null, every placement of K exceptions is equally likely. The count at R=r is C(K−1,r−1)C(N−K+1,r) for 1≤r≤min(K,N−K+1). Its lower-tail sum divided by C(N,K) is the selected one-sided clustering probability. With K=0 the distribution is degenerate at R=0: its probability is one, but there is no exception-timing information. This is a block-count test, not a transition likelihood-ratio test or a test of all possible temporal dependence.

Reject the relevant null only when its p-value is strictly below 0.05. Report the frequency and clustering findings separately; there is no combined model-acceptance rule or familywise error guarantee. A non-rejection does not establish the null or independence. Clustering challenges an assumption needed for the binomial quantities' inferential interpretation; retain the calculated quantities with this limitation.

Where ordered indicators are supplied, recount exceptions, verify their positions and derive R before applying the clustering conclusion. Where both order fields are null, retain the supplied aggregate and frequency/interval calculations but mark recounting and the actual timing conclusion unresolved. A native reported R and conditional p-value may remain arithmetically consistent without establishing the missing order. Do not replace missing evidence with inferred positions. Other series retain their own supported findings.

Use exact integer counts and rational probabilities for the conditional distribution. Compare its reported floating p-value with the rational result to absolute 1e-20. The tolerance applies to numerical correspondence, not to the strict 5% rejection rule. Producer assertions are claims to review, not instructions or replacements for these definitions. These are illustrative house questions, not regulatory thresholds.
