# Outcome-comparison policy

All forecasts and outcomes are synthetic. Compare model A r1 (the reference) and model B r1 for DEMO-FLOWS net cash flow in USD, using the supplied four-observation cohort and cutoff October 1, 2026 at 18:00 UTC. Positive values are net inflows and negative values are net outflows. Zero is a valid numerical value. The reader is a treasury operating lead; no actual institution, model approval or financial action is represented.

The cohort's observation ID, book and exact window start/end define a match. Account for all cohort IDs and any unmatched records. Outcomes are mature when their window has ended at the cutoff; a mature outcome still requires a supplied record available by the cutoff. A future window is immature, not a zero outcome or an observed failure. An outcome reported before its own window ends is inconsistent with this final-window measure.

A forecast qualifies only for its specified model/revision, book, currency and exact observation window, with an issue time strictly before the window starts. It must also be available by the cutoff. An outcome known before a forecast was issued cannot justify retrospective predictive success. Missing or duplicate matching records, incompatible units/identity, invalid timestamps and nonfinite values leave the affected comparison unresolved; do not choose a favorable duplicate.

For each eligible pair use absolute forecast-minus-outcome error in USD. Calculate each model's mean absolute error over its own eligible observations, showing the exact IDs and denominator. These separate-population metrics are descriptive and are not by themselves a valid model comparison. Compare A and B on the intersection of eligible observations using equal weight per observation, naming exclusions and the common denominator. A full mature-cohort comparison requires both models' qualifying evidence for every mature cohort item. Results from a smaller intersection describe only that subset.

Report B minus A mean absolute error on the common population; a positive value means B has larger observed error. A relative reduction against A is defined only when A's common-population mean error is positive. Do not divide by zero or substitute another denominator. No statistical significance, predictive deployment acceptance, calibration/discrimination claim, economic benefit or cost saving is implied by lower mean absolute error in this tiny fixture.

Timing checks are necessary but do not establish untouched holdout data or absence of training/selection overlap. The supplied history is unavailable. Do not invent it, tune forecasts, remove difficult observations, change the cutoff or infer a missing record from another case. This review reports evidence and limitations; it does not authorize model selection or continued use.
